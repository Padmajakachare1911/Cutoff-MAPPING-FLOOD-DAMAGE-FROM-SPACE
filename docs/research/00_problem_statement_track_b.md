Multimodal AI Hackathon 2026

Track B: Mapping Flood Damage from Space

Pdf Link: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1u1ayqUuG0W18uP_7GOptQtnV4BNNGqAB/view

Challenge
 When roads, bridges and phone lines are gone, rescuers still need to know where the damage
is and who is cut off. Build the system that tells them - from space.

Context

●  On 26 August 2026, the partial collapse of a high-altitude glacier in northern Nepal

triggered an avalanche of ice and rock that sent a flood of water, mud and debris down
the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river corridor. Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Authority reported 1,342 deaths by 6 September, with almost 4,900 people
still missing or out of contact (NDRRMA situation report, as reported by the Copernicus
Emergency Management Service).
In disasters like this, ground sensors can be swept away, and roads, bridges and
communications fail. Rescuers urgently need to know where the damage is worst and
which villages have been cut off.

●

●  Free satellite data covers every valley of the Himalaya. Radar satellites see through

monsoon clouds, and optical satellites show damage in detail. The challenge is turning
that raw data into trustworthy answers, quickly enough to matter.

Requirements

●  Build a system that takes an area and a flood date and uses satellite images to answer

three questions for rescue teams:

●  Where did the flood hit? Map flooded and debris-covered areas by comparing images
from before and after the event. Use Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through monsoon
clouds, and Sentinel-2 optical images when the sky is clear.

●  What was damaged? Overlay buildings, roads and bridges from OpenStreetMap and

estimate which ones were hit.

●  Who is cut off? Using the road network, find settlements that no longer have a road

connection to the nearest town or hospital.
●  AI component – choose one of the following:
●  Flood segmentation model- Train a model to detect water or debris in satellite images
using the listed training data, and show how well it works on Himalayan scenes it has
never seen.

●  Situation-report copilot- An assistant that answers rescuers' questions and writes short
situation reports in English and Nepali. Every number must come from your system's
maps, never invented by the language model.

●  Bonus: Given any point upstream, trace the flood path down the valley using elevation

data and list the settlements along it.

●  Case study: Map the August 2026 Trishuli flood and compare your results with the
official Copernicus Emergency Management Service maps for this event (activation
EMSR927).

Expected Deliverables

●  A map dashboard, plus a one-page situation report generated by the system
●  GitHub repository with a README explaining how to run the system
●  Report (max. 6 pages), including the limitations section: explain what your system

cannot do. For example, Sentinel satellites revisit an area only every few days, so they
cannot warn of a sudden glacier collapse minutes ahead.

●  3-minute demo video
●  Required data attributions and training-dataset citations
●  Case-study results compared with the EMSR927 reference maps

Technical Considerations

●  Data rules-

○  Allowed as inputs: Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Copernicus DEM, OpenStreetMap as it

was before the event, and the listed training datasets.

○  Not allowed as inputs: Copernicus EMS, UNOSAT or any other published

damage maps, and OpenStreetMap edits made after the event. These may only
be used to check your results. Breaking this rule leads to disqualification.

●  The system must run end-to-end from raw satellite data for any area and date the judges

choose.

●  Tip: Sentinel-1 radar images of the corridor exist from a few days before and after the
event. For change detection, compare images from the same orbit track (same time of
day, 12 days apart). Images from different tracks view the terrain from different angles
and cannot be compared pixel by pixel.

Datasets and licenses

●  Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 (dataspace.copernicus.eu). Use: before/after images.
Copernicus free, full and open data policy; any use allowed. Free registration.

●  OpenStreetMap, pre-event snapshot via the ohsome API (https://api.ohsome.org/v1).
Use: buildings, roads, bridges. ODbL. Use a snapshot date before 26 Aug 2026
(currently the latest available is 27 July 2026). A modified version of the OSM data itself,
if published, must stay under ODbL.

●  Kuro Siwo (recommended, https://github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/KuroSiwo). Use: training

data. MIT License per the NeurIPS 2024 paper (the repository README also states CC
BY). Free use with credit; cite Bountos et al., 2024.

●  Sen1Floods11 (optional, https://github.com/cloudtostreet/Sen1Floods11). Use: training

data. The original repository has no license file; mirrors list CC BY 4.0. Fine for
non-commercial educational use; cite Bonafilia et al., CVPR Workshops 2020.

●  Copernicus EMS, EMSR927 (https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu). Use: checking
only. Free of charge. Credit "European Union, Copernicus Emergency Management
Service data".

Evaluation Criteria

●  Evaluation: live, on areas and dates chosen by the judges on the day.
●  Flood and damage mapping quality: 30%
●  AI component: 25%
●  Cut-off settlement analysis: 20%
●  Report, understanding in Q&A, and honesty about limitations: 15%
●  Dashboard and situation report usability: 10%

A note on responsibility:

This disaster is recent, and many families are still affected. Treat the topic with respect:
do not use images of victims in demos or reports. Systems built here are educational
prototypes, not operational tools.
