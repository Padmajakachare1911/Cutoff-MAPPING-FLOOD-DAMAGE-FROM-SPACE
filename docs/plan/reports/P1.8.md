# P1.8 — Demo AOIs + experiments

## Non-Trishuli 2026 flood AOI: Euphrates at Deir ez-Zor, Syria (chosen 2026-10-12)
- **Source (the plan's first-named list):** Copernicus GFM news item 247,
  ["Flooding Along the Euphrates River in Syria, May 2026"](https://global-flood.emergency.copernicus.eu/news/247-flooding-along-the-euphrates-river-in-syria-may-2026/)
  (published 2026-06-10). It is also on ReliefWeb (UNFPA flash update "Euphrates floods Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor,
  25 May – 6 June 2026"; WFP satellite flood-extent assessment, June 2026).
- **Event:** releases from the Euphrates (Tabqa) dam after a very wet 2025–26 season. River level +4 m; WFP reports
  the river growing from 60.9 km² (13 May) to 188.7 km² (30 May). About 27,084 people affected and 9,625 displaced
  in Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor; the worst impacts were from Deir ez-Zor city southward.
- **AOI:** `deir_ez_zor` = **40.05,35.25,40.25,35.40**, the Euphrates floodplain around Deir ez-Zor city
  (EPSG:32637, 1838 × 1686 px).
- **Event datetime:** `2026-05-25T00:00:00Z`, the start of the UN-reported flood window (25 May) and the IFRC event
  date.
- **Why this one:**
  1. A real 2026 flood, so it is outside Kuro Siwo.
  2. It is the opposite regime to Trishuli: a flat floodplain (DEM 192–321 m, slope median 1°, 78 % of pixels
     HAND < 10 m) vs a Himalayan gorge. That tests generalisation and P2's D9 lowland fallback (there is no
     HAND > 100 m terrain).
  3. The CDSE STAC shows dense same-track coverage (three satellites: S1A, S1C, S1D).
  4. Pre-event OSM (snapshot 2026-07-27) is irrelevant to a May event, so P3/P4 must pass an earlier `--osm-snapshot`
     (e.g. `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`). **Hand-off note for P3/P4.**
- **Same-track pair (live STAC, `select_pairs`):** track **123 descending**, post
  `2026-05-26T03:25:39Z` (S1C), pre 05-15 (S1D), 05-14 (S1C), 05-03; coverage 1.0, lag 27.4 h. Other valid sets:
  43A (post 05-26 15:16), 50D (post 05-27 03:18). The 123D S1A passes are split into two half-frames (coverage ≈ 0.5
  each), so they are rejected by the 0.8 coverage rule. The flood peaked later (about 30 May), and the earliest-post
  rule takes 05-26 by design.
- The GFM/ReliefWeb/WFP pages were used **only to choose the AOI and date**. None of them is a pipeline input (D13).

## Experiments
- **E3c** (`docs/results/E3c.md`): **σ⁰ part run.** Median |ΔdB| on stable terrain is 0.59 (VV) / 0.58 (VH) dB,
  p90 1.75 / 1.72 dB, so the [TARGET] ≤ 0.5 dB is narrowly missed. At 50 m block means it is 0.25 / 0.24 dB, so the
  difference is mostly pixel-scale. **Downstream run too:** flood-mask (classes 1–2) IoU 0.735 on tiny support (49 px);
  hazard 1–4 IoU 0.313, because full mode's lower stable σ (0.90 vs 1.31 dB) flags about 2.4× more rule pixels.
- **E5** (`docs/results/E5.md`): **run.** `stable_steep_fpr` is 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ (same-track) vs 0.295 (cross-track); flood
  area 0.005 vs 87.3 km²; stable σ 1.31 vs 14.71 dB. **H3 holds.**
