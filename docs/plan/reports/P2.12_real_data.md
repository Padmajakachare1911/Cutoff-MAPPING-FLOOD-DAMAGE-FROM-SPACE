## Findings
1. **The model does not transfer to either real scene.** That is the honest C2 result, and it confirms the warning from E2 (Kuro Siwo cannot measure terrain outside lowland Europe/Asia).
   - **Trishuli (gorge):** p_flood is ~0 everywhere, including the river itself (S2 sees it; the model calls 0.9 % of S2 water "water"). The channel is 5–10 px wide at 10 m and sits between steep, layover-prone walls, unlike any Kuro Siwo patch. The scene is also brighter than the training data (+0.9 to +1.3 std), from slopes facing the radar.
   - **Deir ez-Zor (desert floodplain):** the model calls 95 km² of dry desert "permanent water" and puts its flood scores (0.3–0.9) on desert in the south-east, not on the flooded riverbanks (p_flood on S2 water: median 0.016). Bare desert is very dark in VH (−1.4 training std, about 5 dB below the Kuro Siwo mean): no vegetation to scatter the signal back, so it looks like water. Kuro Siwo has almost no desert.
   - **It is not a pipeline error.** P1's E3c measured the Sentinel Hub and SNAP backends at ~0.2 dB apart on stable terrain, far below these offsets. Speckle filtering is on (P1 U2). The same pipeline reproduces E5 exactly.
2. **The change-rule layer is what catches the real hazard**, as designed (D9):
   - Deir ez-Zor: 4.2 km² of radar decrease (6.3 km² after the gating fixes) along the Euphrates banks (`dVV` panel).
   - Trishuli: the **Bhote Koshi corridor is visible** as a line of coherent change in the `dVV` panel. 9.4 % of valley-floor pixels (HAND ≤ 10 m) change by more than 3 dB, against 4.5 % on stable terrain (HAND > 100 m): a 2× enrichment, of which 0.24 km² passes the 3σ rule (σ 1.31 dB). It is weak; the 57.5 h lag may have let the water recede before the post scene.
3. **The no-false-alarm side holds on Trishuli:** `stable_steep_fpr` 2.1 × 10⁻⁶, no observation 1.59 km² (shadow/layover).
4. **"Permanent water" was badly wrong in both scenes** (radar back-slopes on Trishuli, desert on Deir ez-Zor). It is display-only, since P3's exposure and access use `HAZARD_CLASSES` (1–4), but a dashboard showing 30 % of a demo AOI as lakes would mislead. Fixed in gating (below).

## Gating fixes (branch `p2/P2.8-permanent-water-terrain`, contract text amended)
No new thresholds; both reuse existing evidence, following operational practice (Copernicus GFM and OPERA DSWx-S1 apply terrain/shadow exclusion to reference water, not only to floods).
- **Rule 4:** permanent water needs flat, low terrain, the same test rule 3 uses for floods (slope ≤ 15°, HAND ≤ 30 m).
- **Rule 5:** permanent water that clear-sky S2 contradicts (clear, no water) returns to its rules 1–3 class. S2 already downgrades model floods this way.

| Permanent water (km²) | Original | With both fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Trishuli north | 9.77 | **0.06** |
| Deir ez-Zor | 95.25 | **5.33** (S2 sees 12.0 km² of water) |

Flood classes 1–2 are unchanged in both scenes. Rule detections that permanent water used to hide reappear (Deir ez-Zor decrease 4.19 → 6.31 km², increase 1.88 → 1.98 km²; Trishuli +0.002 km² each).

**Disclosure:** both fixes were motivated by these runs. They change only the display class 5, reuse existing thresholds, and were not chosen to improve any evaluation number. E4 (EMSR927) compares flood extent, which they do not affect. They are included in the v1 submission integration; the project freeze is still pending.

## For the report (P4)
- State plainly: **the U-Net is strong on Kuro Siwo (test IoU 0.689) but does not detect the Trishuli or Deir ez-Zor floods on its own.** The system's flood signal in those scenes comes from the same-track change rule, gated by terrain and S2. This is a real result about domain shift (gorges, deserts), not a failure to hide.
- Keep labelling `p_flood` a "model score".
- E4 will measure what the rule layer is worth on Trishuli.
